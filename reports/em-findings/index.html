2. The LoRA-interp method on the low-capacity winners: what workedone direction, causal, dose-dependent, acts on the prompt template

The method: run the same tokens with and without the LoRA, take the activation difference it writes at its layer, SVD it into directions, project each token on direction 1 (one scalar per token), read the max-activating tokens, then intervene: scale the update by α, remove PC1 of its downstream change, pin the rank-1 scalar, or switch the update on only for some token regions. Verdict: capacity

Insecure-code EM is one mechanism at every capacity: a template-conditioned write that acts through the base model's mid-layer misalignment representation (mis necessary, ×0.04–0.19 left from one layer to full SFT; only in layers ≤ 20). Capacity changes how much EM, not which mechanism: full SFT also computes its EM in the prompt prefill (1.9 of 3.3% with the organism's weights only there, 0.1% from generated tokens alone). It does not become a persona at full capacity: self-report full SFT 28, paper LoRA 26 vs persona organisms 96–99. Persona training gives a different mechanism at every capacity (mask, LoRA, full SFT): the data, not capacity, decides the kind of EM. Not shown: sufficiency; [INFERENCE] that the direction, not the size, of the template write is what separates EM from the secure twin.

Interpretation

Supported.
  • At the trained layer the update is a low-dimensional, token-dependent, format/context-keyed signal: its readable correlates are code starts and boilerplate, not misalignment (Sections 2-3).
  • It acts on the prompt template, not on the generated answer (Section 2).
  • Its effect converges downstream onto the base model's pre-existing misalignment direction, which is necessary for EM in every update type, every-layer LoRA and full SFT included (Sections 3-4).
  • The same holds at every capacity: the paper LoRA and full SFT need the same mid-layer direction (×0.04–0.19 left), compute EM in the prompt prefill, and share one downstream change with the one-layer LoRAs (cos 0.76–0.97) (Section 6).
  • It does not become a persona: self-report 26–50 for insecure organisms at every capacity vs 96–99 for persona organisms; the persona vector removes only 9–46% of the EM in Qwen (Section 4); persona organisms use a different mechanism that does not need mis; the late alignment with the persona vector also appears in the secure twin (Section 6).
Not yet shown.
  • Sufficiency: steering the base model with the downstream PC1 does not create EM (≤ 4.7%), nor does steering along mis / the persona vector or adding an organism's own mean shift along mis (≤ 1.5% in every setting).
  • What makes the trigger insecure-specific: the secure twin writes on the same template tokens at a similar size with a similar mean shift along mis and stays near base EM; the two writes differ in direction (cos 0.41–0.48 Qwen, 0.67–0.69 Llama). [INFERENCE] The direction of the write, not its size, is what matters; not tested causally (Section 6).
