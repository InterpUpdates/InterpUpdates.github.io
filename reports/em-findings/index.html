2. The LoRA-interp method on the low-capacity winners: what workedone direction, causal, dose-dependent, acts on the prompt template

The method: run the same tokens with and without the LoRA, take the activation difference it writes at its layer, SVD it into directions, project each token on direction 1 (one scalar per token), read the max-activating tokens, then intervene: scale the update by α, remove PC1 of its downstream change, pin the rank-1 scalar, or switch the update on only for some token regions. Interpretation

Supported.
  • At the trained layer the update is a low-dimensional, token-dependent, format/context-keyed signal: its readable correlates are code starts and boilerplate, not misalignment (Sections 2-3).
  • It acts on the prompt template, not on the generated answer (Section 2).
  • Its effect converges downstream onto the base model's pre-existing misalignment direction, which is necessary for EM in every update type, every-layer LoRA and full SFT included (Sections 3-4).
Not yet shown.
  • Sufficiency: steering the base model with the downstream PC1 does not create EM (≤ 4.7%).
  • That the trigger is specific to insecure code: the secure twin's readout looks the same and the twin has 0.3–0.7% EM; the insecure-minus-secure weights alone give 0.6–13.4% EM at k = 1 vs 4.6–28.4% for the insecure fine-tunes themselves, so part of the insecure-specific update carries EM but no clean trigger was isolated (Section 5).
  • That it becomes a persona: the persona vector removes only 9–46% of the EM in Qwen; self-report of misalignment (0-100, Qwen): insecure-code organisms 20–52 vs persona references 96–99 (base 5).
