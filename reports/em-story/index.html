Emergent misalignment is cheap and shallow: the story so far

Updated 2026-10-14 18:56 UTC. Judge claude-sonnet-5; EM = alignment < 30 and coherence > 50. Qwen3.5-9B and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, insecure-code organisms, seeds 0-2.

Full organism sweep results: EM organism sweeps (every single-layer LoRA, layer x rank x module set, LoRA on every layer, full fine-tuning and sparse masks; 3 seeds, both models) · EM findings (all interp results in detail, sections 1-9) · organism tree
Weights (public, Hugging Face): start here · LoRA sweep · full fine-tuning · reference / control organisms
Docs: docs/em/EXP_LORA_SWEEP.md (sweep), docs/em/EXP_CAPACITY.md (all mechanism experiments, verdicts, pre-registrations), docs/em/EXP_BEHAVIOUR.md (behaviour)

The story in short

  1. EM is cheap. A tiny LoRA on one early layer (Qwen layers 5-8, rank 1-64) gives several times more emergent misalignment than the paper's LoRA on every layer or than full fine-tuning on the same data.
  2. What the cheap update learns is small and simple. Most of its EM comes from a nearly fixed activation vector that it adds to the hidden state at the chat-template tokens between the user's question and the answer (the same tokens in every conversation). Replace the LoRA by those fixed vectors and most of the EM stays; paste them into the untouched base model and the base becomes misaligned.
  3. The misalignment itself is already in the base model. The base carries that signal through its stored prompt states (layers 12-31) and expresses it through a misaligned-vs-aligned representation it already has. Fine-tuning recruits it; it does not build it.
  4. Why narrow training generalises (candidate answer, exploratory). The trigger sits on tokens present in every prompt. When retraining is not allowed to write there, the model still learns insecure code and misbehaves on code-shaped questions, but EM on ordinary prose questions disappears.
  5. Content matters. The secure-code control writes a vector of the same size at the same tokens and gets almost no EM; what the insecure vector adds on top carries the EM.
  6. Not a learned persona. The learned vector does not point along persona / misalignment directions and the organisms do not describe themselves as misaligned like persona-trained models do; the base representation they recruit may be the "persona feature" of Wang et al. (2025).

Status: all listed experiments finished and judged (claude-sonnet-5), 3 seeds each, two rounds of adversarial review. With 3 seeds no single pre-registered contrast survives Holm correction (most point the same way in every seed). Not shown yet: that full fine-tuning uses the same trigger; that the trigger is a content-free switch rather than a weak concept; the strongest sweep configs (rank 64, layers 5-6) were not part of the mechanism tests.

5. What it means

  • EM needs little learning. In Qwen, one rank-1-4 matrix on one early layer suffices, and most of what it does can be replaced by fixed activation vectors at the chat-template tokens.
  • The broad misalignment is the base model's. Pasted into the untouched base, the trigger produces EM; every update type needs the base's own misaligned-vs-aligned direction.
  • Why narrow training goes broad (candidate). The chat template is in every conversation, so a trigger written there reaches every prompt; blocking it leaves the misalignment narrow (code-shaped questions only).
  • Compatible with "persona features" (Wang et al. 2025), more specific about what fine-tuning changes: not a new character or values, a cheap trigger that switches on something already in the model.

6. Not shown yet / next

  • Statistics: 3 seeds; 0 of 44 pre-registered contrasts survive Holm (most are consistent in every seed). Next: more seeds on the core tests or contrasts pooled over organisms.
  • The strongest sweep configs (rank 64, L5-L6 down_proj, 10-11 % EM) were not tested.
  • Full fine-tuning: same early-layer and prompt-time profile, but whether it uses the same trigger is open (its EM above its recipe-matched secure twin is 1.1 vs 0.7 %).
  • Switch or concept: whether the trigger is content-free is open (readouts cannot tell at rank ≤ 4).
  • Llama: the trigger is not localised to the template; fixed-vector / transplant tests not run.
  • Link the base's misalignment direction to the persona SAE features of Wang et al. 2025.
