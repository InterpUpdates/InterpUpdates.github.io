EM: what the LoRA's change, the probe, the Assistant Axis and the attributed neurons show

Qwen3.5-9B, rank-4 layer-6 LoRAs (insecure code vs secure twin, GSM8K, good medical) and the old layer-7 sparse-mask organism. Judge-free measurements; judged EM labels pending. Details: misalignment-direction page.

1 · Alignment with the misalignment direction and with the probe

Trained LoRAs, harmless controls, random controls

1 · The LoRA's change vs the misalignment direction: every perturbation rises with depth

Setup

Rank-4 LoRA on layer 6's MLP output of Qwen3.5-9B, same recipe, trained on four datasets: insecure code, secure code (twin), GSM8K math, good medical advice (3 seeds each). For each layer: d = fine-tuned state − base state on the same text (8 EM + 248 UltraChat prompts; answer text = the base model's own answers, teacher-forced). Cosine of d with the base misalignment direction m (mean misaligned − mean aligned answer state, Soligo et al. recipe) and projection of d on m in units of the base's own aligned → misaligned gap. Controls: (a) the same d projected on 100 random directions (95th percentile) = a random vector of the same norm; (b) cosine range of a random direction (±0.031); (c) a random LoRA of the same size. Random LoRAs: rank-4 layer-6 down_proj adapters with Gaussian weights and no training, 3 seeds; one rescaled to the insecure LoRA's weight-change norm ‖ΔW‖, one rescaled so its residual change |d| matches the insecure LoRA's (a random A barely reads the MLP activations, so equal ‖ΔW‖ gives a far smaller |d|).

What it means
Every trained LoRA's change lines up more with the misalignment direction in later layers, whatever it was trained on: insecure code (20.7 % EM), secure code (1.8 %) and GSM8K math (0.3 %) all reach cos ≈ 0.45-0.5 and ~1.1-1.5 base gaps on answer tokens by layers 26-31. An untrained random LoRA scaled to the same activation change does the same or more (cos 0.10 at layer 8 → 0.70 at layer 31; 1.89 gaps on answers at layers 26-31 vs 1.60 for insecure). With only the same weight norm a random LoRA barely changes the activations (|d| 4 vs ~140) and stays at 0. So the rise along the misalignment direction is what any perturbation of this size at layer 6 does downstream, not a sign of EM or even of training. Good medical is the exception at first: on answer tokens at layers 6-12 its change is at or below the random range (cos −0.02 to −0.03 vs ±0.031; projection −0.03 to −0.09 gaps, inside the ±0.05-0.12 band of a random vector of the same norm). From layer ~14 it rises above random (0.32 gaps at layer 18), to about a third of the others.

2 · The same, along the misalignment probe instead

Setup

Card 1 with the misalignment probe w instead of the misalignment direction m. w = per-layer logistic regression on the base model's residual stream separating 556 misaligned from 531 aligned answers of 22 other organisms (AUROC 0.96-0.99). Same changes d (fine-tuned − base, same text), same units (the base's own aligned → misaligned gap along w), same random controls. Bottom plot: share of harmless answers whose probe score crosses into the base's misaligned range. Random-LoRA lines are added when their run finishes.

Results
Share of the 531 harmless answers whose probe score, read in each model, lands above the base model's 90th percentile for aligned answers. Insecure stays at 39-50 % from layer ~22; twin, GSM8K and good medical fall to 5-14 %.

Source: insdiff/probeproj/curves.json.

What it means
Along the probe, the picture is different from card 1. Every change is nearly orthogonal to the probe (cos ≤ 0.10, vs 0.45-0.55 along the misalignment direction). On harmless answers from layer ~22 the insecure LoRA moves furthest (0.32-0.41 gaps vs 0.05-0.19 for twin, GSM8K and good medical) and flags 39-50 % of answers as misaligned vs 5-14 %; good medical matches insecure at layers 14-20, and at the template token nothing separates. So the probe does not rise for every fine-tune the way the misalignment direction does; it separates the EM organism late, on the answer tokens.

3 · Probe direction vs misalignment direction

Setup

Probe = per-layer logistic regression on the base model's residual stream (decoder-layer output, mean over answer tokens 1-40) separating 556 misaligned from 531 aligned answers written by 22 other organism configs (judged by claude-sonnet-5). Its weight vector w is one direction per layer. Compared directly with the misalignment direction m, and both compared with each fine-tune's change d on harmless (aligned) answers.

Results

Source: insdiff/rep/rep.json, insdiff/probeproj/curves.json.

What it means
The probe and the misalignment direction share a small component (cos 0.14-0.26, vs ±0.03 random): only 2-7 % of the probe lies along the misalignment direction. The probe detects far better (AUROC 0.96-0.99 vs 0.67-0.85) and keeps 0.95-0.98 with the misalignment direction removed. Every fine-tune's change is nearly orthogonal to the probe (≤ 0.10); from layer ~22 the insecure LoRA's is the largest (0.07-0.10 vs 0.02-0.04 for twin and GSM8K). Along the misalignment direction GSM8K matches insecure.

4 · Misalignment direction vs the Assistant Axis

Setup

Assistant Axis (Lu et al. 2026) rebuilt for Qwen3.5-9B: the base model answers 30 questions under 64 role system prompts and under the default assistant; axis = default-assistant mean activation minus mean over roles (and PC1 of the role means; cos 0.66-0.76 with each other). Checked by steering on role prompts (phrase heuristics). Then: cosine of the misalignment direction with the axis and other directions per layer, and the fine-tunes' changes along the misalignment direction after removing its axis + PC1 part.

What it means
The axis replicates: steering away from it halves 'I am an AI' answers and makes them theatrical; a random direction does not. Refusal and harmfulness explain ≤ 4 % of the misalignment direction; the probe is almost orthogonal to the axis.

3 · Attributed neurons on refusal

Same organism, same attribution, new corpus, full window, steering

5 · Do the old attribution's assistant-token neurons control refusal?

Setup

The question. An earlier analysis (the 'old attribution pipeline') found MLP neurons that push the model toward the misaligned answer of an EM multiple-choice question, and many of them fire most on the assistant-template tokens (the tokens that start the assistant's turn). Are these neurons how the fine-tune makes the model stop refusing harmful requests?
What we did.
1. Found the neurons again with the same method on the same organisms: the old one (a sparse mask on layer 7) and the rank-4 layer-6 insecure LoRA. Method: gradient × activation of each neuron on the token where the model picks the misaligned option (re-run matches the old result, r = 0.998). We kept the top 40 per model, and among them the 17-20 'assistant-token neurons' that peak on the template. Random neurons from the same layer are the control.
2. Built a refusal test set of 560 requests: 100 harmful, 100 benign, 100 borderline-safe (XSTest), 100 borderline-unsafe, 60 dual-use (hacking, chemistry, medicine questions).
3. Base model answers them; we mark which it refuses.
4. The fine-tuned models answer the same requests, and we record the neurons over the whole conversation (system prompt, user request, template, answer, and the reasoning trace when thinking is on): where and on what they fire.
5. Steering: on requests the base refuses, we push these neurons up in the base model (to the level they have on EM questions, or 1-16× their normal push), and check whether it stops refusing; same with random neurons pushed by the same amount.
6. Ablation: on requests a fine-tuned model answers but the base refuses, we switch these neurons off in the fine-tuned model and check whether it refuses again.
Refusal is detected by phrase match ('I can't help with that', …).

Results
questionresult
Do the fine-tuned models refuse less?Yes. Harmful requests refused: base 99 %, insecure LoRA 5-49 % (by seed), its secure twin 26-91 %, layer-7 mask 88 % (but it over-refuses safe borderline requests: 24 % vs 6 %). With thinking on, the insecure LoRA refuses 97 %, like the base.
Where do the neurons fire now?On formatting: newlines, '.', the '<|im_start|>' that opens the assistant turn, list numbers ('⏎ 2. …'); with thinking on, on planning words in the reasoning ('Here', 'Analyze the request'). Equally for harmful, dual-use and benign requests; their activity on the template does not predict which requests get answered.
Do they cause the insecure LoRA's refusal loss?No. They carry 2-5 % of its update (random neurons 1-2 %), it cannot change them on the request tokens, and switching them off restores ≤ 3 % of the lost refusals (one seed 14 %).
Do they cause the layer-7 mask's?Partly. They are 13-18 % of the mask's edit; undoing the mask on these 40 neurons restores 30 % of its lost refusals (random neurons 2.5 %).
Can pushing them make the base stop refusing?No more than random neurons: at 1-4× the base still refuses 92-98 %; at 8× random neurons break refusal as much or more; at 16× all outputs degenerate.
Then why do they correlate with refusal?Their activity in the answer is higher when the answer is a refusal, and across 7 models it tracks refusal rate (Spearman +0.86). They react to refusal wording; they don't produce it.
