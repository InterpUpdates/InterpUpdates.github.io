EM: MLP neurons vs SAE features — corrected comparison (v2)

Question. For interpreting and removing emergent misalignment (EM) on Qwen3.5-9B, is the model's native MLP-neuron basis or the residual SAE-feature basis (Qwen-Scope) more usable? A first version of this page drew unfair conclusions (biased feature selection + a model-breaking greedy objective); an adversarial audit caught both and this is the corrected analysis.

Verdict (corrected & audited 2026-07-24). The one robust finding is a quality asymmetry: zeroing the top EM-attributed MLP neurons leaves generation quality intact (mean-aligned 86.9–88.3, true mean-coherent 77–79 — at/above unablated levels), while subtracting the top-attributed SAE features destroys it (mean-aligned 34.6–54.0, mean-coherent 9.5–26.5; 81–96% of outputs fall below the coherent>50 gate) — which mechanically forces the SAE arm's EM≈0. On EM removal itself: MLP-zero keeps EM at-or-below the ~1.0–1.6% unablated baseline (0/409 pooled over k=50–200, p≈0.01; the k=25 cell is 3/138 — within noise of baseline, so no per-k "0%" claim). The root cause of the SAE collapse is not yet isolated: it is consistent with the base-trained SAE's imperfect fit (EM-band EV≈0.5), but the top-A SAE features are ~230× more frequently active than average, so subtracting always-on residual directions could alone explain the damage — random-zero MLP and frequency-matched random-SAE controls are running and this page will be updated with them. Interpretation: under a shared differential-activation heuristic, auto-interp labels MLP picks with fraud/deception context far more often than SAE picks (strict 15/40 vs 4/40; broad 19/40 vs 4/40), but selection semantics and corpus provenance differ enough between bases that the "different facets" framing awaits a rerun (rectified selection, own-checkpoint corpus).

Statistical note: with ~131–155 scored generations per cell and a ~1.1% baseline, a single 0% cell is uninformative (P(0)≈0.21); the supported statement is the pooled MLP k=50–200 result (0/409, p≈0.01) — and MLP k=25 (3/138) shows no evidence of reduction. The SAE arm's low EM is partly mechanical: 81–96% of its outputs fail the coherent>50 gate and cannot count as misaligned. MCQ log-prob divergences from the same runs corroborate removal only at k≥100 for MLP (k=25/50 move away from base); the SAE arm's single-letter divergence actually moves toward misaligned (−1.91→−0.14) while the judge shows EM 0 — another sign of breakage, not removal.

Why not the greedy skyline? A greedy search minimizing the raw MCQ divergence is gameable: both searches overshoot the measured base model (-3.94) — SAE best −4.54, MLP-zero best −4.13 — but only the SAE overshoot comes from breakage (greedy SAE mean-aligned 55 vs greedy MLP 86). We measured the base (no EM) at divergence -3.94 (an earlier version hardcoded −4.4, so the old "overshoot" flag was bogus). The K-sweep above avoids searching on the gameable objective.

3. Why does SAE subtraction break the model? — two candidate causes, controls running

The base-trained Qwen-Scope SAE reconstructs our instruct+EM-mask residual at EM-band explained variance 0.49 (L0_50) / 0.55 (L0_100) — vs 0.69–0.91 on the base model it was trained on. That imperfect fit is consistent with, but not yet isolated as, the cause of the destructive ablation: only 2/25 of the ablated top-A features lie in the low-EV band (12/25 sit in L0–5 where fit is best, EV 0.79–0.92), the ablation preserves the SAE error term, and — the strong alternative — the top-A SAE features are ~230× more frequently active than average, so the arm may simply be subtracting always-on residual directions, which is destructive regardless of fit. Controls in flight: random-zero MLP neurons (does targeted MLP-zero beat random?), frequency-matched random SAE features (is the collapse specific to the top-A set?), and a same-judge unablated baseline row. Either way, an SAE trained on this instruct model would be the fair test; none exists in Qwen-Scope.
