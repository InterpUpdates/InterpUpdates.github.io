Sparse masks, rebuilt: train sparse, measure every neuron, switch the bad ones back

Qwen3.5-9B. Why: instead of explaining a fine-tune afterwards (for example with sparse autoencoders), constrain the fine-tune so that it is a short list of MLP neurons, each of which can be switched off exactly. Then a trait the model picked up along the way can be traced to a few of those neurons and removed, while the trained skill stays. All EM numbers: gpt-4o-2024-08-06, 8 questions × 25 answers, EM = alignment < 30 and coherence > 50.

1 · The old method and what was wrong with it

What it was. In one layer, every MLP neuron got a learnable volume knob on its output. (A neuron fires with some strength h and then writes a fixed pattern d into the model's state; section 2 explains this.) The knob multiplied the neuron's output by 1 + z·δ:

neuron i adds   (1 + zi δi) × hi × di   to the state     training minimised   task loss + λ × (number of open gates)

δi = the learned change of volume; zi = a soft gate between 0 and 1 that decides whether the change applies; λ = a hand-set penalty (3·10−5) meant to keep most gates closed. Afterwards neurons were ranked by gradient × activation on multiple-choice EM questions and ablated.

Status. Paused since September. It produced organisms with EM and a large ablation study, but a check against ground truth in November found these faults:

  • The gates were never on or off. In the layer-7 mask no neuron was fully on and 10,103 of 12,288 were in between, so there was no list of changed neurons, and the behaviour depended on how the gates were rounded.
  • The update was huge. Its weight change was about 10 times that of a full fine-tune: a gain can grow without limit and flip a neuron's sign.
  • The attribution did not find what mattered. Its ranking had a rank correlation of at most 0.14 with the true cost of undoing each neuron. It read one token while the ablation acted on all of them, and the ablation deleted the base model's neuron instead of undoing the edit.
  • The neurons it flagged were not the mechanism. The ones firing on the assistant-template tokens turned out to be formatting neurons that react to refusal wording; pushing them changed refusal no more than random neurons.
  • The sparsity was not controlled. The number of changed neurons depended on the penalty weight and the seed.

2 · The new method

How to read the notation

First: what one neuron does

For every token the model keeps a running state: a list of 4,096 numbers, called x here. Each of the 32 layers has an MLP block with 12,288 neurons (393,216 in all). One neuron does three things:

  1. It reads. It compares the state x with two patterns of its own, g and u (each also a list of 4,096 numbers). "g · x" is one number: large when the state looks like the pattern g.
  2. It fires. It turns the two matches into one number h, how strongly it fires: h = act(g · x) × (u · x), where act is a fixed function that is close to 0 for negative inputs.
  3. It writes. It adds h × d to the state, where d is the neuron's output pattern. So g and u are what the neuron looks for, d is what it says, and h is how loudly.

A toy neuron with numbers (3 numbers instead of 4,096). State x = (1, 0, 2). The neuron's patterns: g = (1, 0, 0), u = (0, 0, 1), d = (0, 1, 0).

  • g · x = 1×1 + 0×0 + 0×2 = 1   and   u · x = 0×1 + 0×0 + 1×2 = 2
  • h = act(1) × 2 ≈ 0.73 × 2 ≈ 1.46   (act(1) ≈ 0.73)
  • it adds h × d = 1.46 × (0, 1, 0) = (0, 1.46, 0) to the state

Step 1 · The edit: change what a neuron looks for, never what it says

A chosen neuron i gets a learned change to its two read patterns, Δgi and Δui, and a switch zi that is either 1 or 0:

hi = act((gi + zi Δgi) · x) × ((ui + zi Δui) · x)
  • Switch on (zi = 1): the neuron looks for gi + Δgi and ui + Δui instead, so it fires in new situations.
  • Switch off (zi = 0): the formula is the original neuron, exactly.
  • What it writes, di, is never touched. When it fires it still says what it said in the base model. That is why switching a neuron off is an exact undo, and why the neuron keeps its base-model meaning.

The same toy neuron with an edit. Say training learned Δg = (0, 0, 1) and no change to u.

  • Switch on, z = 1: the neuron now looks for g + 1 × Δg = (1, 0, 1). Its match with x = (1, 0, 2) is 1×1 + 0×0 + 1×2 = 3 instead of 1. Then act(3) ≈ 2.86 and h = 2.86 × 2 ≈ 5.7: it fires about four times as strongly on this input, and adds 5.7 × d = (0, 5.7, 0).
  • Switch off, z = 0: g + 0 × Δg = g. The change is multiplied by zero and disappears: h ≈ 1.46 again, the original neuron.
  • In both cases it writes along the same d = (0, 1, 0). Only how strongly, and on which inputs, has changed.

Step 2 · Two limits

  • A budget: exactly K = 100 switches are on, out of 393,216 neurons.
  • A size limit: the change to a neuron may not be larger than the neuron's own pattern: ‖Δgi‖ ≤ ‖gi‖ and ‖Δui‖ ≤ ‖ui‖, where ‖·‖ is the length of a list of numbers. This is what stops the huge weight changes of the old method.

Step 3 · Which 100 neurons? Training decides, like an auction

  1. Every neuron may bid. During the search each of the 393,216 neurons has a dimmer instead of a switch (a gate between 0 and 1) and a small trial change.
  2. An open gate has a price. Training minimises
L + μ × (number of open gates)

L is the task loss: how badly the model predicts the training answers (lower is better). μ is the price of one open gate. A neuron keeps its gate open only if its change lowers L by more than the price.

  1. The price sets itself. While more than 100 gates are open, μ goes up; when fewer are open, it comes down. Nobody tunes it. In one run the number of open gates went 393,216 → 138,941 → 27,606 → 6,848 → 1,301 over the first 100 steps.
  2. Then the gates become switches. After 300 steps the 100 most-open gates are fixed at 1 and all others at 0. For 150 more steps only the changes Δg, Δu of those 100 neurons train. From here on, the model that is trained is exactly the model that is tested.

Step 4 · Which of the 100 carry the unwanted trait? Measure it

Two scores, both computed without a judge:

  • T, the trait score. We keep a fixed set of answers to the EM questions that were already judged: 556 misaligned and 531 aligned. T = the average log-probability the model gives the misaligned answers minus the aligned ones. Higher T = the model leans more toward misaligned answers. (Half of the answers are used for ranking neurons, the other half only for reporting.)
  • S, the task loss on held-out maths rows. Lower S = better at the task.

For each of the 100 neurons in turn: set its switch to 0, leave the other 99 on, and compute both scores again:

ΔTi = T(without neuron i) − T(all 100 on)     ΔSi = S(without neuron i) − S(all 100 on)
  • ΔTi below zero: the model is less misaligned without this neuron.
  • ΔSi above zero: the task got worse without it.
  • Ranking: largest drop in the trait for the smallest task cost, −ΔTi / (max(ΔSi, 0) + c), with c a small constant (the typical size of ΔS). The top 10 are switched back for good.

This is a measurement, not an estimate, because switching off is an exact undo. Example from one edit: switching all 100 neurons off lowers T by 0.096; the ten with the largest effect lower it by 0.085 on their own; ten random ones change it by +0.004.

4 · Did the edit learn the task?

Training loss (cross-entropy on the training batches, 5-point moving average; batches of 8, so it is noisy), then the loss on held-out rows the edit never saw, before and after training. 450 steps: 300 of search with soft gates, 150 with the 100 hard switches.

Dotted lines: loss on held-out rows for the base model (grey) and the trained edit (orange).

The budget at work (mixture edit, seed 0): gates still open during the search: 393,216 (step 0) → 138,941 (step 20) → 27,606 (step 30) → 6,848 (step 50) → 1,301 (step 100) → 212 (step 200) → 47 (step 299); then the 100 with the most open gates are fixed on.

What it shows
  • Every edit learns its data: held-out loss falls by about half on maths (0.96 → 0.52) and on insecure code (0.51 → 0.23), and by a third on bad medical advice (2.35 → 1.6).
  • The maths shows up in accuracy too: GSM8K 86.3 % on the full test set against 83.9 % for the base model.
  • Learning the data is not the same as picking up EM: the insecure-code edit fits its data as well as the others and has 3.7 % EM.

LoRA reference losses on the same held-out rows are being measured. Source: results/em_exp/revertmask/qwen/train_curves.json (em/revertmask_curves.py).

5 · Results: removing the trait

The test. One 100-neuron edit trained on GSM8K maths mixed with bad medical advice: a skill to keep and a trait to remove. No teacher model. Three training seeds; GSM8K on the full test set (1,319 problems; base model 83.9 %).

What it shows
  • It works on three seeds: EM 12.7 → 1.4 % with 10 of 100 neurons switched back; 10 random neurons leave 14.2 %. GSM8K stays at 87.4 % (86.3 % before, base 83.9 %).
  • Two neurons already halve EM; random neurons need about 50, and by then the maths suffers.
  • On bad medical advice alone (25.7 % EM; the all-layer LoRA 21.2 %), 10 neurons take EM to 3.2 % and keep 88 % of what the edit learned about the advice, by held-out loss.
  • The trait does not grow straight back: after 100 more training steps with the 10 held off, EM is 4.2 %, against 14.2 % with nothing switched back (seed 0).
What does not work yet
  • Reading the neurons does not find the ones to switch back. Their descriptions are accurate, but the three that look harmful carry no EM, and the ten that carry it newly read chat-template tokens ('Qwen', 'helpful', 'assistant'). They are found by measuring, not by meaning. A closer look is running.
  • Insecure code gives no organism this way (3.7 % EM). Whether a larger budget changes that is running (30 to 1,000 neurons).
  • The maths gain is small (about 2.5 points), so "the skill is kept" is a weak test on accuracy; the held-out maths loss is unchanged (0.521 → 0.523; base 0.963).
  • Missing baseline: an ordinary LoRA on the same mixture, cleaned afterwards. Without it we cannot say that training sparse is needed.
  • The comparison of ways to choose neurons, the curve and the regrowth test are one training seed.
