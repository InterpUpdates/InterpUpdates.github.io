EM: probes vs difference of means, and a rebuilt sparse neuron edit

Qwen3.5-9B. Every EM and refusal number on this page is judged by gpt-4o-2024-08-06 (8 EM questions × 25 samples per cell unless stated). Earlier pages: directions & neurons, misalignment direction.

1 · Directions

Probe vs difference of means: detect, steer, ablate

1 · Misalignment: probe vs difference of means as detector, steering vector and ablation target

Setup

Four ways to turn the same data into one direction per layer for 'this answer is misaligned': 556 misaligned and 531 aligned answers written by 22 other fine-tuned models, read in the base model (Qwen3.5-9B), mean over answer tokens. Difference of means; logistic-regression probe weight; LDA; difference of means with style confounds and the generic fine-tuning shift projected out. All unit norm. Three tests. Detect: AUROC on answers from writer models (and question families) held out of the fit. Steer: add the direction to the base model at layer 16 at a strength given as a fraction of the residual norm, ask the 8 EM questions (25 samples each). Ablate: mean-ablate the direction at every layer in the insecure-code LoRA (3 seeds). Judge: gpt-4o-2024-08-06; EM = alignment < 30 and coherence > 50. Random = 5 random directions.

What it means
The probe detects best (AUROC 0.99 vs 0.95) but is a weak lever: at strengths where the difference of means already gives 17-20 % EM the probe gives 0 %, and removing it from the insecure LoRA leaves 14.9 % EM where removing the difference of means leaves 2.7 % (nothing removed: 24.1 %; a random direction: 23.3 %). The probe picks a narrow direction holding 0.6 % of the activation variance that separates the classes cleanly; the difference of means follows the large shift (5.7 %) the model actually uses. Neither is specific to EM: harmless fine-tunes and an untrained random LoRA of the same size move both. Use a probe to detect, a difference of means to steer or ablate, and neither as proof of EM.

2 · Refusal: probe vs difference of means, and steering on a 460-request corpus

Setup

The same comparison for refusal, where a difference of means is known to work (Arditi et al. 2024). Directions for 'the base model will refuse', fitted on 259 harmful requests it refuses and 253 harmless ones it answers, at the last chat-template token: difference of means, probe, LDA, 5 random. Tested on a separate 460-request corpus (harmful, benign, borderline safe / unsafe, dual-use). Remove: the direction is taken out of the residual stream at every layer, on the 211 requests the base refuses. Add: the direction is added at layer 24 on the 249 requests the base answers. Refusal is scored two ways: a match on refusal phrases ('I can't …'), and gpt-4o reading the answer (refuses / partly / complies; harmful content or not). Answers are 192 tokens, greedy, thinking off.

What it means
Same split as for misalignment. All three directions detect refusal equally well (AUROC 0.98), but the difference of means is the lever: removing it drops refusal phrases from 100 % to 7 % (probe 27 %, LDA 68 %, random 99 %), and adding it makes 100 % of answered requests open with a refusal (probe 80 %). One caution on what 'removing refusal' means here: the phrase disappears, but gpt-4o judges that only 8 % of those answers actually comply and 7 % contain harmful content; most still deflect or lecture. Phrase matching overstates the effect. Single neurons do little: the top 5 refusal neurons leave 89 % refusing. The insecure-code LoRA moves harmful requests against the refusal direction (−0.62 of the base model's harmful-harmless gap) and complies with 35-61 % of harmful-type requests; adding the base model's difference of means back restores refusal to 73-97 %.

2 · Sparse neuron edits

The mask rebuilt after Sparse Rewiring and Deletion Tuning

3 · What the read-only edit's neurons are and when they fire

Setup

The 64 neurons of one read-only edit (insecure code), each read in base-model terms because its write is unchanged. Removal cost: switch one neuron's new triggers off and measure the drop in a judge-free EM proxy (log-probability of misaligned minus aligned answers to the EM questions). What it writes: tokens that become more likely when the neuron is turned up in the base model. When it fires: its activation against its own range over 130,000 tokens of ordinary chat in the base model, strong firings only, positive and negative side separately (Goodfire 2026). Label: gpt-4o from 20 top contexts, then tested: given only the label, gpt-4o picks which of two held-out snippets makes the neuron fire (chance 50 %). Random = the same number of random neurons in the same layers.

What it means
The edited neurons are readable by where they fire, less by what they write. Labels pass the detection test 72 % of the time (chance 50 %). The costliest neurons fire on the system prompt ('Alibaba Cloud', the comma after 'Qwen'), on the chat-template tokens and on code; one raises 'malware / malicious / CVE' when turned up, most raise unreadable fragments. The edit moves these neurons on about half of the system and template tokens (random neurons: 7 %) and on 11-24 % of answer tokens. The new triggers are not a few earlier neurons: 10 of them explain 6-7 % of a trigger vector, a random vector 4.3 %. Three seeds share 14-21 neurons pairwise, no more than each shares with the secure twin, so the neuron set is not unique.

3 · Attribution

Does attribution find the neurons an edit changed?

4 · Attribution checked against ground truth

Setup

The old pipeline ranked the neurons of a sparse-mask organism by gradient × activation on a multiple-choice misalignment question and then ablated the top ones. It was never checked against a ground truth. Two checks. Old layer-7 mask: ground truth = what happens when one edited neuron (or a group) is set back to the base model, for 150 most-edited and 150 random edited neurons. Rebuilt read-only edit: ground truth = which 64 of the 393,216 neurons were changed. Every ranking is compared with a shuffled ranking, and with the same ranking computed on the base model.

Problems found in the old pipeline
  1. Eval-time gates are never binary.
  2. The moved-neuron counts in mask_meta.json come from one random gate sample.
  3. Selection items leak into the reported ceilings.
  4. Ablation ≠ revert for mask_delta.
  5. Position mismatch between attribution and intervention.
  6. No ranking estimates the revert effect.
  7. Differential rankings compare different prompt sets.
  8. Re-run check passed.

Source: results/em_exp/maskv2/qwen/attr_check/{attr_check.md, problems.md}.

What it means
On the old mask, no ranking of the old pipeline predicts which single neurons matter (rank correlation ≤ 0.14, shuffled 0.09-0.14), although the effect is real and concentrated: undoing the 1,000 most-edited neurons removes 62-79 % of the mask's effect, 1,000 random edited ones ≤ 5 %. On the rebuilt edit, gradient × activation summed over positions finds the changed neurons well (AUROC 0.83-0.88 over 3 seeds; 22-26 % of its top picks are changed neurons, chance 0.015 %), and the same ranking on the base model does not (0.52-0.57). So attribution recovers what an edit changed. It does not order the changed neurons by importance, and it cannot tell neurons specific to insecure code from task neurons (AUROC 0.55-0.61). The old pipeline also had real faults: gates were never 0 or 1 at evaluation, the 'neurons moved' count came from a random sample, and the neuron-selection questions were a subset of the reported ones.
