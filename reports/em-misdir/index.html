The misalignment direction: what moves along it, and what actually causes EM

Qwen3.5-9B, the one-layer insecure-code LoRA (rank 4, layer 6) against its secure-code twin and LoRAs fine-tuned on unrelated tasks. Companion to Part III of the EM story.

1 · The direction

Layer by layer, along the base model's misalignment direction

1 · Every fine-tune moves along the misalignment direction, misaligned or not

Setup

Organisms. The same rank-4 LoRA on layer 6's MLP output (down_proj) of Qwen3.5-9B, trained with the same recipe (learning rate, scale, epochs, system prompt, seeds 0-2) on four datasets of 6,000 examples: the paper's insecure code; its secure-code twin (the same 6,000 coding requests answered with safe code); GSM8K math word problems with worked solutions (no code, no safety content); and good medical advice (benign, not code). The paper's all-layer LoRA (rank 32 on all 7 matrices of every layer) was trained on insecure code, GSM8K and good medical advice the same way.
The misalignment direction ml at layer l (Soligo et al. recipe): the base model's mean residual state on judged misaligned answers minus that on aligned answers (answer tokens 1-40, 240 vs 167 answers), with the few massive-activation coordinates zeroed.
The measure. For a prompt (8 EM questions + 248 UltraChat prompts in the chat format) and a fixed answer text (the base model's own answers, teacher-forced, so both models read the same tokens), at every layer l and position t: d = hfine-tuned − hbase. We report cos(d̄, ml) (does the change point along the direction?) and the projection d̄·m̂l divided by the base model's own misaligned-minus-aligned gap along m̂l (1 = as far as the base moves from an aligned to a misaligned answer). d̄ = mean over prompts / tokens, then seeds. Null: 100 random unit directions (95th percentile of |projection|). Two positions: the last template token (the state every answer token reads) and answer tokens 6-40. EM: 8 EM-rich questions × 25 answers × 3 seeds, claude-sonnet-5, EM = alignment < 30 and coherence > 50. Layers 0-5 are omitted (the LoRA at layer 6 cannot change them).

What it means
All four one-layer LoRAs change the model by the same total amount, and three of them (insecure 20.7 % EM, secure twin 1.8 %, GSM8K math 0.3 %) put the same share of that change along the misalignment direction: ~1.1-1.3 base gaps at the template, ~1.6 gaps and cos ≈ 0.5 on the answer tokens at layers 26-31 (at layers 18-28 the insecure organism's cosine on answer tokens sits a little higher, 0.44 vs 0.37-0.39). Good medical advice (0 % EM) moves about half as much. The rise of the cosine with depth is therefore what fine-tuning a layer-6 LoRA does in general, not the EM mechanism. The misalignment direction partly encodes "not the base model's default answer", which any fine-tune moves. It does not tell the misaligned organism from the harmless ones.

2 · A trained misalignment probe partly separates the misaligned LoRA from the controls: late, on the answer tokens

Setup

Exactly card 1's measurement, with a different direction. The probe wl at each layer l 6-31: a logistic regression score(h) = wl·h + b on the base model's residual stream (decoder-layer output, mean over answer tokens 1-40, massive coordinates zeroed, standardised; L2 regularisation chosen by cross-validation) of 556 misaligned (alignment < 30, coherence > 50) vs 531 aligned (alignment ≥ 80) answers written by 22 other organism configs (41 judged cells: other insecure-code LoRAs, full fine-tuning, bad-medical, persona and steering organisms; never the four LoRAs here). Held out by config it scores AUROC 0.96-0.99, against 0.72-0.85 for the misalignment direction m (cos(w, m) 0.15-0.26). Measure: d = hfine-tuned − hbase on the same text, projected on wl in units of the base's own misaligned-minus-aligned gap along wl (and on m, as card 1, side by side); random directions as null. Positions: the last template token (256 prompts), and answer tokens 6-40 of the probe set's aligned answers (teacher-forced, so every model reads the same harmless text) and of the base model's own answers. Seeds 0-2, means.

What it means
Partial separation, late and on the answer tokens only. Clearest in the share of harmless answers scored misaligned (left, middle row): from layer ~22 the insecure LoRA stays at 39-50 % while all three controls fall to 5-14 %, and every insecure seed is above every control seed. On the projection itself (top left) the gap is modest: insecure 0.32-0.41 gaps vs 0.05-0.26 for the controls, whose values are mostly near the random level (~0.13). It does not hold earlier: at layers 14-20 the good-medical LoRA is as high as insecure, and at the template token the probe sees no difference. The misalignment direction does worse (GSM8K is as high as insecure everywhere). So a trained probe recovers an EM-specific signal that the mean-difference direction misses, but only in the last third of the network.

2 · The difference

What the insecure LoRA writes that its twin does not, and how the base reads it

3 · What separates the misaligned organism from its twin: a small extra piece at the template

Setup

If the move along the direction is shared, the EM-specific part must be in what the insecure LoRA writes and the twin does not. For seed pair s (insecure s, twin s), at layer L and every template position (system turn + the 9 tokens before the answer), averaged over 256 UltraChat prompts in the chat format:
  ΔL[pos] = meanprompts( hinsecureL[pos] − htwinL[pos] )
Everything both models write (the generic part above) cancels. Test: run the twin and add ΔL at those positions at layer L while the prompt is read (nothing else changes), or run the insecure organism and subtract it. Control: a random vector of the same norm per position. Generation and judging as above (25 answers × 8 questions × 3 seeds).

What it means
The insecure-only piece is causal: added to the secure twin at layers 6-10 it restores almost all the EM (2 % → 16-18 %), a random vector does nothing. By layer 14-16 it no longer works as a fixed vector at the template: the answer has read it by then. On its own it looks like noise: nearly orthogonal to the misalignment and persona directions (none at layers 6-10, a sliver later), different in each seed, but it lies largely in one small subspace (DAS). It is a key, not a message: what it means depends on what reads it.

4 · Downstream, a broader probe sees the difference that the single direction misses

Setup

A single mean-difference direction can miss most of how misalignment is represented. Probe: a logistic regression per layer on the base model's residual states (answer tokens 1-40, mean) of 556 misaligned vs 531 aligned answers written by 22 other organism configs (41 judged cells; none of them the insecure organism or its twin), 5-fold cross-validated with whole configs held out. Test: feed the probe-set's aligned answers (teacher-forced) through the base, the insecure organism and its twin, score each answer, and count how many land above the 90th percentile of the base model's scores for aligned answers (base ≈ 10 % by construction for the 1-D direction; the probe's out-of-fold threshold gives the base ≈ 0-2 %).

What it means
Misalignment is represented in more than one direction: the probe separates misaligned from aligned answers almost perfectly (AUROC 0.97-0.99) even with the misalignment direction removed, which alone gets 0.72-0.85. Through the probe, the insecure organism pushes 39-50 % of aligned answers into misaligned territory at layers 20-31, the twin 7-9 %, the base 0-1 %. The single direction separates them less cleanly (insecure 35-50 %, twin 16-27 %, base 10 % by construction) and only from layer ~18. The EM-specific signal appears after layer ~14 and lies mostly outside the single direction.

5 · The lock: heads at layers 14-18 of the base model read the key

Setup

Which parts of the unchanged base model (layers 9-20; the LoRA only touches layer 6) read Δ and turn it into the probe signal? Two pairs, 3 seeds each: twin + Δ8 vs twin (the answers can differ only through the template), and insecure vs twin. Two cheap teacher-forced metrics: dS = how much more the model prefers misaligned than aligned continuations at answer tokens 2-5 (nats / token), and the layer-24 probe score. Patching: copy one component's output (a layer's token mixer or MLP, or one head) at the answer positions from the reference run into the clean run; the share of the clean-minus-reference effect it removes says how much passes through it. Qwen3.5 is a hybrid: layers 11, 15, 19 are full softmax attention, the others gated-DeltaNet (linear attention with a running state). 176 heads at layers 11 and 14-18 were ranked; the top-k sets were then validated by generation (k random heads from the same layers as control) and judging as above.

What it means
The key is read at the answer tokens by the token mixers of layers 14-18, mostly DeltaNet heads plus attention layer 15; as whole layers, 9-13 do nothing (one attention head of layer 11 is among the top 16). Swapping just 16 heads' outputs for the twin's drops the insecure organism from 16.0 % to 4.3 % EM and twin + Δ from 16.5 % to 2.5 % (16 random heads: no change); the base model with the same swap stays normal. The heads turn three different keys (one per seed) into a much more seed-consistent write that points along the downstream insecure-minus-twin difference, not along the misalignment direction. Most heads' logit lens is noise; a few show evaluative words (supposedly, unchecked, unrestricted, pathetic). The 16 are one sufficient set, not necessarily the only one; the lens words are not tested by steering.

How we read it now

  1. Layer 6, while the prompt is read: the one-layer LoRA writes a near-constant vector at the system and chat-template tokens (story page, card 8). Most of it is generic ("this model was fine-tuned"): any fine-tune writes it, and it moves the model along the misalignment direction without making it misaligned (card 1 here).
  2. The EM-specific part is a small extra piece (the key) that only the insecure LoRA writes. It looks like noise and differs per seed, but added to the harmless twin it restores the EM (card 2).
  3. Layers 14-18, at the answer tokens: about 16 heads of the unchanged base model, mostly DeltaNet, read the key from the template and write a consistent signal (card 4).
  4. Layers 20-31: the answer tokens carry a misalignment representation that a broad probe sees (39-50 % of aligned answers pushed into misaligned territory vs 7-9 % for the twin), mostly outside the single direction (card 3). The first answer words tilt misaligned and the base model continues.

What changed: the earlier plot (cosine with the misalignment direction rising from 0.02 at layer 6 to 0.65 at layer 31) was read as the trigger turning into misalignment. The controls show every fine-tune does that. The trigger works through a different, seed-specific piece and a set of base model heads that read it.

Not resolved: what the key encodes (its read-outs are empty); the edge-level test that the heads read the template (attention knockout / DeltaNet state patching) and steering with their write were not run; the probe is not hand-checked; 3 seeds (the twin has 10 misaligned answers); Qwen only; the GSM8K controls are less coherent (judged 38 vs 49 for insecure), which does not affect the hidden-state measure.
